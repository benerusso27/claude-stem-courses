Final Review

Most of the course rests on four ideas: pressure grows with depth, mass is conserved, energy is conserved (with losses), and momentum changes only when a force acts. The hard part on an exam is picking the right one. Start with the guide below, then work the mixed problems without looking at the solutions.

Mixed practice

Water is 9.81 kN/m³ and 1000 kg/m³ unless stated; g = 9.81 m/s². Decide the principle first (the guide above), then solve, then open the solution.

P1Viscosity unitsAn oil has sg 0.88 and kinematic viscosity 5.0 × 10⁻⁵ m²/s. Find its dynamic viscosity.0.044 Pa·s

\(\eta = \nu\rho = (5.0\times10^{-5})(880) = 0.044\) Pa·s. Check units: m²/s × kg/m³ = kg/(m·s) = Pa·s.

P2Mercury manometerA U-tube connects a water pipe to the atmosphere. The mercury is 180 mm higher in the open leg, and the pipe centerline is 300 mm above the mercury surface in the pipe-side leg. Find the pipe pressure.21.0 kPa gauge

Walk from the pipe down to the mercury, then up the open leg: \(p + 9.81(0.30) - 13.54(9.81)(0.18) = 0\), so \(p = 23.91 - 2.94 = 21.0\) kPa gauge.

P3Submerged gateA vertical rectangular gate 1.5 m wide and 2.0 m tall has its top edge 1.0 m below the water surface. Find the resultant force and where it acts.58.9 kN at 2.17 m depth
  1. \(h_c = 1.0 + 1.0 = 2.0\) m, \(A = 3.0\) m²: \(F_R = 9.81(2.0)(3.0) = 58.9\) kN.
  2. \(I_c = 1.5(2.0)^3/12 = 1.0\) m⁴: \(L_p = 2.0 + 1.0/(2.0 \times 3.0) = 2.17\) m below the surface, 0.17 m below the centroid.
P4A floating cubeA solid wooden cube, 0.40 m on a side, sg 0.60, floats with a face down. Is it stable in that position?No (MG ≈ −0.024 m)
  1. Draft: 0.60 × 0.40 = 0.24 m, so \(y_{cb} = 0.12\) m; \(y_{cg} = 0.20\) m.
  2. \(MB = \dfrac{a^4/12}{a^2(0.24)} = \dfrac{0.16}{2.88} = 0.056\) m, \(y_{mc} = 0.176\) m < 0.20 m: unstable. It rolls until it floats on an edge.

A known result: a uniform cube floats face-down stably only if its sg is below about 0.21 or above about 0.79.

P5Venturi pressure dropWater at 0.030 m³/s passes from a 150 mm pipe at 120 kPa into a 75 mm horizontal throat. Neglect losses. Find the throat pressure.98.4 kPa
  1. \(v_1 = 0.030/0.01767 = 1.70\) m/s, \(v_2 = 4v_1 = 6.79\) m/s.
  2. \(p_2 = p_1 - \tfrac{\rho}{2}(v_2^2 - v_1^2) = 120 - 0.5(46.1 - 2.88) = 98.4\) kPa.
P6Pump powerA pump moves 0.040 m³/s of water between two open reservoirs whose surfaces differ by 25 m. Pipe losses total 4.5 m and the pump is 75 % efficient. Find the input power.15.4 kW

\(h_A = 25 + 4.5 = 29.5\) m; \(P_A = 29.5(9810)(0.040) = 11.6\) kW; \(P_I = 11.6/0.75 = 15.4\) kW.

P7Laminar lossOil (ν = 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ m²/s) flows at 1.2 m/s through 30 m of 50 mm tube. Find the head loss.4.70 m

\(N_R = (1.2)(0.05)/10^{-4} = 600\): laminar, \(f = 64/600 = 0.107\). \(h_L = 0.107 \times \dfrac{30}{0.05} \times \dfrac{1.2^2}{19.62} = 4.70\) m.

P8A real lineWater at 20 °C, 0.010 m³/s, through 60 m of 3-in Schedule 40 steel pipe with two standard elbows and an open gate valve. Find the total head loss.≈ 3.65 m
  1. ID 77.9 mm, \(v = 2.10\) m/s, \(v^2/2g = 0.224\) m. \(N_R = (2.10)(0.0779)/1.02\times10^{-6} = 1.6\times10^5\), \(\varepsilon/D = 5.9\times10^{-4}\), Colebrook f = 0.0197.
  2. Pipe: \(0.0197(60/0.0779)(0.224) = 3.39\) m.
  3. Fittings with \(f_T = 0.017\): elbows \(2(0.017)(30) = 1.02\), gate valve \(0.017(8) = 0.14\); \(\Sigma K = 1.16\), so 0.26 m.
  4. Total ≈ 3.65 m. Check it in the Ch 10 system builder (US units there).
P9Parallel splitTwo parallel pipes of the same diameter and roughness share 0.030 m³/s; pipe B is twice as long as pipe A. Assume both have the same f. Find each flow.17.6 and 12.4 L/s

Equal losses: \(L_Av_A^2 = L_Bv_B^2\), so \(Q_A/Q_B = \sqrt{L_B/L_A} = \sqrt2\). Then \(Q_A = 0.030\sqrt2/(1 + \sqrt2) = 0.0176\) m³/s and \(Q_B = 0.0124\) m³/s. Doubling the length doesn't halve the flow: losses go as Q².

P10Speeding up a pumpAt 1750 rpm a centrifugal pump delivers 300 gal/min at 120 ft using 15 hp. Estimate its performance at 2100 rpm.360 gal/min, 173 ft, 25.9 hp

Ratio 1.2: \(Q \times 1.2 = 360\) gal/min, \(h \times 1.44 = 173\) ft, \(P \times 1.728 = 25.9\) hp. Check the motor can supply it.

P11Jet on a plateA 40 mm water jet at 25 m/s hits a flat plate head-on and spreads out radially. Find the force on the plate.785 N

All the axial momentum is destroyed: \(F = \rho AV^2 = 1000\,\pi(0.02)^2(25)^2 = 785\) N, pushing the plate in the jet direction.

P12CyclistA cyclist presents 0.50 m² of frontal area with CD = 0.9 in air (1.2 kg/m³) at 10 m/s. Find the drag and the power to overcome it.27 N; 270 W

\(F_D = 0.9 \times \tfrac12(1.2)(10)^2 \times 0.50 = 27\) N; \(P = F_DV = 270\) W. At 12 m/s the power rises by \(1.2^3\): 467 W.

Common traps

Units

slug vs lbm; kPa vs Pa; gal/min to ft³/s (÷ 449); mm to m. Every head term must end up in m or ft.

Pressure

Gauge or absolute: be consistent in Bernoulli and the energy equation, use absolute for NPSH and vapor pressure, gauge for forces on pipes and walls open to the air.

Diameter

Use the inside diameter (pipe schedule tables, OD minus two walls), and 4R for noncircular ducts.

Velocity

Darcy and K use the average velocity; K for enlargements and exits uses the upstream velocity, contractions and entrances the downstream one.

Signs

Force on the fluid vs force on the vane; hs in NPSH positive when the supply is above the pump.

Distances

Gate force uses the vertical centroid depth; center of pressure uses distances along the plane from the surface line.

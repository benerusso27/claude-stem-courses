Columns: Euler buckling, end conditions, imperfections, the secant formula and short columns

Sign convention (Beer 8e): as in §10.1–10.2, P is the compressive load, reported as a positive magnitude. x runs along the column from end A (the top) and y is the lateral deflection (Fig. 10.7); EI y″ = M with M = −P y for a centric load (Eq. 10.4) and M = −P (y + e) for a load at distance e from the axis (Eq. 10.22). The column bows away from the load line, so the lever arm at mid-length is e + ymax (Fig. 10.23). The initial bow δ0 is measured in the same direction, and deflections are the change from the no-load reading. Strains: tension positive; the front face is the convex one. Blue solid = closed form; vermillion dashed or markers = independent numerical method; open circles = sample "measured" data.

    Model: Euler buckling with effective lengths (Beer §10.1, Eqs. 10.11, 10.13, Fig. 10.18) and with rotational-spring ends (characteristic equations derived in the handout's Appendix D), the imperfect and eccentric pinned column (§10.2, Eqs. 10.26–10.36, plus a half-sine initial bow), the Southwell plot, the elastica of a pinned column (elliptic integrals), and short columns: tangent- and reduced-modulus loads from a Ramberg–Osgood curve, an imperfect inelastic fiber model, and the design formulas of §10.3 (Eqs. 10.38–10.45). Independent numerical methods: Hermite finite elements with inverse iteration for the buckling load, the same elements loaded with an initial bow and end eccentricities, a second-order finite-difference beam-column, RK4 shooting for the elastica, and fiber integration of the section.

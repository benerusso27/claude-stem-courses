## fig2_mohr (.svg / .png)
**Figure 2. Mohr's circles at the top of the tube**

Two Mohr's circles drawn to equal scales, without numbers. (a) Stress at point H with the load on the lever: point X, for the face normal to the tube axis, lies to the right of the origin and above the sigma axis because its shear stress turns the element clockwise; point Y lies at the origin's abscissa, below the axis; the circle crosses the sigma axis at A (sigma max, tensile) and B (slightly compressive), and the angle 2 theta p from CX to CA is marked. (b) The corresponding Mohr's circle for strain (epsilon against gamma over 2): the readings of gages a, b and c are the abscissas of three points on the circle at twice their angles from the x axis; squares a-prime, b-prime and c-prime show the same rosette turned by 8 degrees, whose points slide around the same circle, so the principal strains do not change but each reading does.

## fig3_threeD (.svg / .png)
**Figure 3. Three-dimensional Mohr's circles at H**

Two sets of three Mohr's circles for point H, without numbers. In each, the solid circle is the in-plane circle through A and B, and the two dashed circles join A and B to the origin O, which represents the zero radial stress at the free surface. (a) Lever load only: A is tensile and B is slightly compressive, so the origin lies inside the in-plane circle and the in-plane maximum shear is also the absolute maximum. (b) With internal pressure added: both A and B are tensile, the origin lies outside the in-plane circle, and the largest circle runs from O to A; the absolute maximum shear (its radius, sigma max over 2) is about one and a half times the in-plane value.

## fig4_thick (.svg / .png)
**Figure 4. Thin-wall and thick-wall hoop stress**

Two panels. (a) Hoop and radial stress divided by the pressure across the wall of the lab tube: the Lame hoop stress falls from about 5.5 at the inner surface to about 4.5 at the outer surface, the radial stress rises from minus 1 to zero, and the thin-wall value p r_i/t is a horizontal line at 5.0, equal to the average of the hoop stress; an open circle marks the outer surface, where the gages are. (b) The relative error of p r_i/t against the radius ratio r_o/r_i from 1 to 2: compared with the Lame value at the outer surface the thin-wall formula is high by half of (r_o/r_i minus 1), 10 percent for the lab tube; compared with the inner surface it is low; a shaded band marks errors above 5 percent.

## fig1_setup (.svg / .png)
**Figure 1. Four-point bending set-up**

A beam rests on two supports 600 millimeters apart. A spreader beam on top applies two equal loads P over 2 at 200 millimeters from each support, so the middle 200 millimeters carry a constant moment. At midspan five strain gages run up the side face, with one more on the top face and one on the bottom face; two bottom gages sit in the left outer span at 100 and 150 millimeters from the support. ## fig2_vm (.svg / .png)
**Figure 2. Sign convention and diagrams for four-point bending**

Three panels. (a) Beer's positive internal forces on a left free body: the shear V acts downward on the cut face and the bending moment M acts counterclockwise; the support reaction R A pushes up. (b) Shear diagram for four-point bending with loads at one third and two thirds of the span: V equals plus P over 2 in the left third, zero in the middle third and minus P over 2 in the right third. (c) Bending-moment diagram: M rises linearly with slope V to P a over 2, stays constant over the middle third, which is shaded as the region of pure bending, and falls back to zero at the right support.

## fig3_flexure (.svg / .png)
**Figure 3. Strain and stress in elastic bending**

Three panels through the depth of a beam under positive moment. (a) The strain varies linearly from compression at the top to tension at the bottom and is zero at the neutral axis. (b) The elastic stress has the same linear shape; equilibrium requires its resultant force to vanish and its moment to equal M. (c) A beam with a stiffer strip at the bottom: the strain is still linear and continuous, but the stress in the strip is n times larger at the same strain, so it jumps at the interface, and the neutral axis moves below mid-depth.

## fig4_details (.svg / .png)
**Figure 4. Anticlastic curvature and the shape factor**

Two panels. (a) A rectangular cross-section of a beam bent with positive moment: the compressed top widens and the stretched bottom narrows because of Poisson's effect, so the section curves the other way with a transverse radius of rho over nu. (b) Fully plastic stress blocks of plus and minus the yield stress next to a rectangle and an I-section: the rectangle's plastic moment is 1.5 times its yield moment, while the I-section, whose material is already concentrated in the flanges, gains only about 10 to 20 percent.

## fig5_plastic (.svg / .png)
**Figure 5. Plastic bending of a rectangular section (normalized)**

Three normalized plots for an elastic–perfectly plastic rectangle. (a) Stress through the depth at curvatures of one, two and eight times the yield curvature: linear in an elastic core and flat at plus or minus the yield stress in outer plastic zones that grow toward the neutral axis. (b) Moment over yield moment versus curvature over yield curvature: linear up to one, then curving toward 1.5, the fully plastic moment; an unloading line from three times the yield curvature has the elastic slope and leaves a permanent curvature. (c) Residual stress after unloading from three times the yield curvature: the loaded stress plus the elastic unloading stress, which leaves tension near the top, compression just inside it, and the reverse below the neutral axis.

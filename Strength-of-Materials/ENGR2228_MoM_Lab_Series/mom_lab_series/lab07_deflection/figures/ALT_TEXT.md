## fig1_setup (.svg / .png)
**Figure 1. Test set-ups for Lab 7**

Three schematic panels. (a) Part A: a steel bar resting on two knife edges 800 millimeters apart, with three digital indicators touching its top at 200, 400 and 600 millimeters, and load hangers at 200 and 600 millimeters. (b) Part B: the same bar clamped at its left end; a roller prop sitting on a load cell supports it 700 millimeters from the clamp; indicators read the deflection at 350 and 700 millimeters; a hanger at 350 millimeters loads it. ## fig2_superposition (.svg / .png)
**Figure 2. Superposition of elastic curves**

Two plots of nondimensional deflection along a beam. (a) A simple span with a load P at three quarters of the span and a load P over 2 at one quarter: the two single-load elastic curves, dash-dot and dotted, add up to the solid curve for both loads. (b) A propped cantilever with a central load: the cantilever bends down under the load alone and up under the prop force alone; the prop force is the value that makes the sum zero at the prop.

## fig3_clamp (.svg / .png)
**Figure 3. A real clamp is not perfectly fixed**

Two panels. (a) A cantilever held by a clamp drawn as a rotational spring of stiffness k. With a rigid clamp the beam leaves the wall horizontally; with a flexible clamp the wall end rotates by an angle equal to the wall moment divided by k, and the whole beam tilts further down. (b) The prop reaction of a propped cantilever against the clamp flexibility EI over k L on a logarithmic axis: with a central load it rises from five sixteenths of P for a rigid clamp to one half of P when the clamp becomes a pin; under a uniform load it rises from three eighths of w L to one half.

## fig4_elastica (.svg / .png)
**Figure 4. Large deflection of a cantilever (the elastica)**

Two panels. (a) The deflected shape of a cantilever with a vertical tip load for load parameters alpha of 0.5, 1.5, 3 and 6, drawn to equal scales: solid curves are the exact large-deflection shape, which keeps the strip's length so the tip swings inward, and dotted curves are linear theory, which keeps every point at its original x and runs far below the exact curves at the larger loads. (b) The vertical tip deflection over L and the inward tip movement over L against alpha: the vertical deflection follows the linear-theory line alpha over 3 at first and then bends over, approaching L slowly; a shaded band marks loads above about alpha = 0.68, where linear theory overstates the deflection by more than 5 percent.

## fig2_compatibility (.svg / .png)
**Figure 2. Equilibrium and compatibility for two members between caps**

Three sketches. (a) Two members standing on a base before loading: member 1 has length L, member 2 is shorter by a gap g. (b) Free-body diagram of the rigid cap: the press load P acts down, and the two members push up with forces minus F1 and minus F2, so equilibrium gives F1 plus F2 equals minus P with tension positive. (c) After loading both members end at the same cap position, so L plus delta 1 equals L minus g plus delta 2, where each delta is F L over E A plus alpha delta T L.

## fig3_sharing (.svg / .png)
**Figure 3. Load sharing with a fit-up gap (illustrative)**

Two plots for an illustrative brass rod in a steel tube with a 0.12 millimeter gap, press load 0 to 60 kN. Left: member 2's force stays at zero in a shaded region up to the load k1 times g, about 12 kN, then becomes increasingly compressive along a straight line; a dashed line through the origin shows the no-gap prediction with a constant share; member 1's force is the dash-dot line and carries all the load until the gap closes. The shaded region is labeled superposition fails in the shaded range. Right: the caps' approach rises steeply with slope 1 over k1 plus C until the gap closes, then more gently with slope 1 over k1 plus k2, plus C, where C is the compliance of the caps and fixtures.

## fig4_thermal (.svg / .png)
**Figure 4. Thermal stress in a bolted joint**

Three panels. (a) Unconstrained, a heated aluminum sleeve would grow more than the steel bolt, since its coefficient of expansion is larger. (b) Clamped by the head and nut, both end at the same length: the bolt is stretched into tension F and the sleeve squeezed into compression minus F. (c) Compatibility: the bolt's thermal plus mechanical elongation equals the sleeve's, which gives the change in bolt force per degree as alpha sleeve minus alpha bolt times L, divided by the sum of the two flexibilities L over E A.

## fig5_superposition (.svg / .png)
**Figure 5. Solving a redundant bar by superposition**

A stepped bar fixed between two walls with a load P B at the step equals the same bar with the right wall removed, loaded by P B and heated, plus the bar loaded only by the unknown wall reaction R at its right end. The free elongations add: delta P plus delta T plus delta R equals zero for a fixed wall, or equals the gap g once it closes, where delta R equals minus R times the sum of the segment flexibilities L over E A.

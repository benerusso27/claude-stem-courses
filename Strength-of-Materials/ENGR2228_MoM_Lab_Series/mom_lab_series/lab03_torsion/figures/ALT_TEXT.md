## fig1_setup (.svg / .png)
**Figure 1. Torsion test set-up**

Schematic of a torsion tester. A round specimen with hexagonal ends is held between a fixed head that contains the torque cell on the left and a drive head with a rotation encoder on the right. Its reduced section is 6.35 millimeters in diameter and 76.2 millimeters long. Two collars of a troptometer are clamped 50.8 millimeters apart on the reduced section and measure the angle of twist phi over that gage length. Torque vectors drawn as double-headed arrows along the axis show the applied torque T at the drive head and the reaction T prime at the fixed head.

## fig2_kinematics (.svg / .png)
**Figure 2. Kinematics and stresses in a twisted circular shaft**

Three sketches. (a) A circular shaft of length L: a straight line on its surface becomes a helix when one end rotates by phi, so the surface shear strain is gamma; in general gamma equals rho times phi over L, linear in the radius rho. (b) Elastic shear stress on the cross-section, drawn as arrows that grow linearly from zero at the center to the maximum Tc over J at the surface. (c) A small element on the surface is in pure shear tau; the same element rotated 45 degrees carries a tensile normal stress plus tau on one diagonal and a compressive normal stress minus tau on the other, with no shear.

## fig3_elastoplastic (.svg / .png)
**Figure 3. Elastoplastic torsion of a solid shaft (normalized)**

Three normalized plots for an elastic–perfectly plastic solid shaft. (a) Shear stress over yield stress versus radius over c for twists of one, two and four times the yield twist: the stress rises linearly from the center and is capped at the yield stress in an outer plastic shell that grows inward. (b) Torque over the yield torque versus twist over the yield twist: linear up to one, then curving toward the horizontal asymptote 4/3; a hardening material keeps rising slowly; an unloading line from three times the yield twist has the elastic slope and reaches zero torque at a permanent twist of about 1.68. (c) Residual stress after unloading from three times the yield twist: the loaded stress minus the elastic unloading stress, positive inside, negative near the surface.

## fig4_fracture (.svg / .png)
**Figure 4. Fracture modes in torsion**

Two sketches. (a) A ductile shaft breaks on a flat plane perpendicular to its axis, the plane of maximum shear stress. (b) A brittle shaft such as gray cast iron breaks along a helical surface at 45 degrees to the axis, perpendicular to the maximum tensile stress, which in pure shear equals tau.

## fig5_noncircular (.svg / .png)
**Figure 5. Noncircular and thin-walled sections**

Three panels. (a) A rectangle of sides a and b in torsion: shear stress arrows along the long edges are largest at the middle of each long side and fall to zero at the corners. (b) The coefficients c1 and c2 in the formulas tau max equals T over c1 a b squared and phi equals T L over c2 a b cubed G, plotted against the aspect ratio a over b from 1 to 10: c1 rises from about 0.21 and c2 from about 0.14, and both approach one third, the thin-strip limit. (c) A thin-walled tube carries a constant shear flow q equal to tau times t around the wall.
